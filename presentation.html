05 · What the data refused to say

We tested nine explanations. Here’s what survived.

Held up

  • Imported calories are thinner — in 82% of countries
  • Starch predicts anemia — across countries, after every control
  • Iron form beats iron amount — animal share r = −0.55 vs total r = −0.21
  • Malaria is a separate driver — 6 of 7 trap countries with data have low malaria

Did not hold

  • Imports alone — Belgium & Bahrain both import 96%+; anemia 15% vs 38%
  • Change within a country — Lesotho cut starch fastest, anemia still rose
  • Diet as a 3-year early warning — no signal (p = 0.22–0.72)
  • Food prices as the channel — local prices falling in most trap countries

Trade isn’t the villain. It’s the amplifier.

07 · What to do

Fix the plate where it enters the country.

1

Fortify imported staples at the port

Iron, folic acid & vitamin A in imported wheat, rice, maize and oil. Three exporters ship 68–78% of traded rice.

GAMBIA · LIBERIA · LESOTHO · DJIBOUTI
2

Rebalance the import basket

Cut tariffs on pulses, dried fish, eggs and milk powder; diversify suppliers. Chicken is set to get cheaper.

TRADE & FINANCE MINISTRIES
3

Protect what’s grown at home

Vegetables, pulses, eggs and small livestock — the denser half in 82% of countries.

AGRICULTURE · WATCH-LIST COUNTRIES
4

Put women 15–49 first

Weekly iron–folic acid via antenatal care and schools; vouchers for iron-rich foods.

HEALTH & SOCIAL PROTECTION
5

Run a yearly starch-trap monitor

Recompute the three conditions every year; review any country within 0.25 SD of the trap.

FAO · WHO · WFP · MOROCCO · CABO VERDE · HAITI
6

Treat the non-food causes

Malaria control and clean water where anemia is high but trade isn’t the route.

GABON · CÔTE D’IVOIRE · NIGER · MOZAMBIQUE
